Here's this week.

What I shipped

Two websites went live. This one, gautamtalks.com, and a rebuild of my engineering portfolio. Both are hand written. No React, no build step, no dependencies except one 3D library.

The thing I'm proudest of is hidden. Click my photo on the homepage and you walk into a low poly version of my room where a blocky version of me wakes up, researches, films a video, edits it, posts it everywhere, and then talks a friend out of quitting. It's about forty seconds long and nobody asked for it.

Nobody asked for it is usually the reason a thing gets remembered.

What broke

Almost everything, in order.

⚡ The animation played fine but nobody could see it, because a title card was sitting on top of the whole thing from the first frame
⚡ The character sat down at his desk and immediately stood back up, because an idle animation was quietly undoing his pose every frame
⚡ I built a signup form, tested it eight times, and locked myself out of my own website with my own rate limiter
⚡ I typed an API key where the whole world could have read it, then had to rotate it

That last one is the one worth talking about.

The thing I actually learned

I pasted a secret key as a command argument instead of into a prompt. Two characters of difference. It meant the key landed in my terminal history file, on disk, in plain text.

It would have been very easy to shrug. The key had existed for about four minutes. Nobody was watching. The realistic risk was close to zero.

I rotated it anyway, and that took ninety seconds.

Here's the maths that made the decision easy, and I think it generalises to a lot more than API keys. Rotating a maybe compromised credential costs you ninety seconds. Not rotating one costs you an incident. Those numbers never get closer together. The moment you find yourself calculating whether it's probably fine, you already have your answer.
